# Light Novel and Illustrated Fiction Guide

## Definition

A light novel is a commercially packaged, accessible novel often combining fast prose, dialogue, strong character hooks, anime/manga-influenced design, and interior illustrations. It is a packaging and audience category, not a single mandatory manuscript format.

## Practical targets

| Component | Working target |
|---|---|
| First volume | 35,000–60,000 words |
| Short volume | 25,000–35,000 words |
| Chapters | 8–12 |
| Color illustrations | 2–4 |
| Black-and-white illustrations | 6–10 |
| Bonus material | 1 side story, glossary, or character file |

## Volume structure

```
Title page
Copyright page
Color character spread
Prologue
Chapters 1–10
Epilogue
Bonus story
Character or terminology index
```

## Volume arc

- Prologue: catastrophe, awakening, death, ritual, or mystery
- Chapter 1: protagonist and immediate problem
- Chapters 2–4: setting, companions, system, or faction
- Chapters 5–7: discovery, betrayal, rival, or escalation
- Chapters 8–10: confrontation and consequence
- Epilogue: new threat or next-volume hook

## Illustration plan

Use illustrations where visual information matters:

1. Protagonist reveal
2. First supernatural transformation
3. Main companion group
4. Antagonist or faction reveal
5. Signature weapon or familiar
6. Mid-volume emotional reversal
7. Final confrontation
8. End-page teaser

## Prose principles

- Open scenes late
- Prefer concrete sensory detail
- Use dialogue to expose conflict
- Avoid explaining the entire world before the protagonist needs it
- Give every supernatural ability a cost, limitation, or emotional consequence
- End chapters with a question, revelation, threat, or decision

## Adaptation readiness

Record scene locations, characters present, action beats, props, sound cues, visual motifs, and emotional turns. This makes the manuscript easier to convert into manga pages, storyboards, audio scenes, or animation episodes.
